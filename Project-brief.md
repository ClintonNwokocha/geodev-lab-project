\# Project brief

**\#\# The question**  
Why are parts of Abuja (AMAC) getting flooded in 2026\.

**\#\# Study Area**  
Abuja Municipal Area Council

**\#\# Why does it matter**  
Abuja, the capital city of Nigeria, has in recent years experienced recurring flash flooding across several of its major districts, including places previously considered low risk, this study is done to understand this flash floods, and proffer solutions to stakeholders (FCT minister, Federal Capital Development Authority, LGA Chairmen and Abuja Environmental Protection Board), to prevent such disasters subsequently and identify where to prioritize resilient structures, green space restoration and drainage interventions.

**\#\# The data needed**

| Category | Dataset | Source |
| ----- | ----- | ----- |
| Rainfall | CHIRPS | UCSB CHC / GEE |
| Topography | Copernicus GLO-30 DEM | GEE / OpenTopography |
| Land cover time series | Sentinel-2 \+ Landsat | GEE |
| Drainage network | OpenStreetMap | osm.org |
| Soil/infiltration | ISRIC SoilGrids | soilgrids.org |
| Building footprints | Open Buildings | OSM |
| Population/exposure | WorldPop | worldpop.org |
| Social media/news images | Manual compilation | — |

**What I would build?**

This study will build an interactive AMAC Flood Risk web-based GIS tool to visualize flood susceptibility, land-use change, population exposure, and drainage infrastructure gaps across Abuja Municipal Area Council, supporting evidence-based prioritization of resilience investments ahead of the 2027 rainy season.
